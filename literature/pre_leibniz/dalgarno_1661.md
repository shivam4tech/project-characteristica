# George Dalgarno — Ars Signorum (1661)

**Gate ruling: PASS (high confidence)** — criteria G1 (representation primitives), G5 (taxonomic organization), G6 (ambiguity reduction), G9 (documented Leibniz engagement). Ruling basis: `gate_rulings.md`.

## Relevance justification

Ars Signorum is a taxonomy-first a priori language: a small set of generic notions partitions the universe of discourse, and every word's sign encodes its position in that partition — taxonomic organization driving character formation, with ambiguity reduced by unique constructed signs. Reception is documented: Leibniz knew and praised Dalgarno's scheme in his discussions of universal characters (New Essays III context; standard in the scholarship), and the modern scholarly edition (Cram & Maat, OUP 2001) treats him as one of the three principal 17th-century philosophical-language designers alongside Wilkins and (retrospectively) Leibniz.
