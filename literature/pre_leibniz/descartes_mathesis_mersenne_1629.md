# Descartes — Mathesis Universalis + Letter to Mersenne, 20 Nov 1629

**Gate ruling: PASS (high confidence)** — criteria G1 (representation primitives), G2 (compositional mechanisms), G7 (formalization of conceptual relations), G8 (machine-relevant design principles), G9 (influence on the characteristica program). Ruling basis: `gate_rulings.md`.

## Relevance justification

The 20 November 1629 letter to Mersenne (AT I 76–88) is the canonical pre-Leibniz statement of the alphabet-of-thought program: decompose all thoughts into simple notions "ordered like numbers," assign characters, and generate everything else by systematic recomposition. Leibniz's characteristica universalis is a direct response to this proposal — he engages it repeatedly and tries to answer the feasibility objection Descartes raised. *Mathesis universalis* (Regulae, Rule IV) supplies the underlying idea of a universal science of order-and-measure. Both are mechanism-bearing, not biographical targets.

## Dating note (honesty flag)

Standard critical edition dates the reply **20 November 1629** (AT I 76–88). One secondary source consulted (Milkov, Paderborn preprint) gives "11 November 1629"; the discrepancy is unresolved here but immaterial to content. Mersenne's own prior proposal was pasigraphic (a general writing understood across languages), per Milkov — Descartes widened it to ideography mirroring thoughts.
