### 1672-1676: Paris Period — Critical Development
| Date | Event | Details |
|------|-------|---------|
| 1672 | Arrives Paris on diplomatic mission | Stays 4 years; meets leading scientists |
| 1672-1673 | Studies Pascal, Descartes, Malebranche, Arnauld | Deepens philosophical/mathematical knowledge |
| 1673 | Demonstrates calculating machine to Royal Society | Elected Fellow 1673 |
| 1674 | *Elementa Characteristicae Universalis* (draft) | Early systematic attempt at universal characteristic |
| 1675 | Discovers differential/integral calculus (independently) | Mathematical breakthrough; influences symbolic thinking |
| 1676 | Returns to Hanover via London | Meets Oldenburg, Boyle, Newton |

**Paris Period Significance:** 
- Intensive work on *lingua universalis* and *characteristica*
- Reads Wilkins (1668) and Dalgarno (1661) critically
- Develops idea of "real characters" — signs that represent concepts directly
- Begins characteristic numbers (numerical representation of concepts)
- Calculus discovery shows power of formal symbolic systems

## Terminology Evolution Timeline

| Term | First Appearance | Evolution |
|------|------------------|-----------|
| *ars combinatoria* | 1666 (title) | 1666: combinatorial art; 1670s: method for concept generation; 1690: includes characteristic numbers |
| *characteristica universalis* | ~1674 | 1674: universal characteristic; 1678: real character system; 1686: linked to infinite analysis; 1700: calculus ratiocinator |
| *lingua universalis* / *lingua generalis* | 1678 | 1678: universal language with real characters; 1680s: merged with characteristica |
| *calculus ratiocinator* | ~1697 | 1697: calculus of reasoning; 1700: numerical decision procedure; later: mechanical reasoning |
| *scientia generalis* | 1677 | 1677: universal science; 1680s: mathesis universalis; later: subsumed by characteristica |
| *alphabet of human thought* | 1670s | Metaphor for primitive concepts; never fixed list |
| *characteristic numbers* | ~1679 | 1679: numerical signs; 1690: prime factorization; 1700: divisibility calculus |
| *real character* | 1668 (Wilkins) | Leibniz adopts/critiques: signs representing things not words |
| *analysis of concepts* | 1686 | 1686: infinite analysis; finite for necessary, infinite for contingent |
| *predicate in subject* (praedicatum inest subjecto) | 1686 | Core truth theory; all true propositions analytic in principle |

## Key Intellectual Transitions

### Transition 1: 1666 → 1670s (Combinatoria → Lingua Universalis)
- **From:** Generating all concept combinations mechanically
- **To:** Assigning unique characters to concepts; classification by genus/differentia
- **Catalyst:** Reading Wilkins (1668) and Dalgarno (1661); sees need for *semantic* not just *combinatorial* system
- **Preserved:** Combinatorial generation as method for discovering complex concepts
- **Abandoned:** Pure formal combination without semantic interpretation
- **New Capability:** Hierarchical classification; real characters

### Transition 2: 1670s → 1686 (Lingua Universalis → Generales Inquisitiones)
- **From:** Building a universal language with fixed taxonomy
- **To:** Metaphysical analysis of concepts and truth; infinite analysis for contingency
- **Catalyst:** Metaphysical development (individual substances, complete concepts); correspondence with Arnauld
- **Preserved:** Predicate-in-subject truth; real characters as expressions of concepts
- **Abandoned:** Hope for finite complete taxonomy; fixed primitive alphabet
- **New Capability:** Theory of contingent truth; infinite analysis; principle of sufficient reason

### Transition 3: 1686 → 1700 (Analysis → Calculus Ratiocinator)
- **From:** Philosophical analysis of truth (infinite, non-mechanical)
- **To:** Mechanical calculus using characteristic numbers (finite, algorithmic)
- **Catalyst:** Need for decision procedure; influence of calculating machine; algebraic thinking
- **Preserved:** Concept containment as truth; intensional semantics
- **Abandoned:** Infinite analysis as method (too ideal); full linguistic expressiveness
- **New Capability:** Arithmetic decision procedure for syllogistic reasoning

### Transition 4: 1700 → 1716 (Calculus → Fragments)
- **From:** Systematic numerical calculus
- **To:** Fragmentary notes; binary arithmetic; metaphysical synthesis
- **Catalyst:** Institutional duties (Berlin Academy); priority disputes; declining health
- **Preserved:** Vision of universal calculus; binary as simplest characteristica
- **Abandoned:** Completing any unified system
- **New Capability:** Binary notation; metaphysical unity (monads express universe)

## Unresolved Problems at Death (1716)

1. **Primitive Concepts:** No fixed alphabet; multiple incompatible lists in fragments
2. **Complete Taxonomy:** Never achieved; classification trees incomplete
3. **Syntax for Propositions:** No grammar for complex propositions in characteristica
4. **Relational Concepts:** No treatment of relations (only monadic predicates)
5. **Negation & Disjunction:** Characteristic numbers only handle conjunction/containment
6. **Quantification:** No universal/existential quantifiers in calculus
7. **Contingency Decision:** Infinite analysis not mechanizable; no finite procedure
8. **Natural Language Interface:** No translation method from natural language to characteristica
9. **Ambiguity Handling:** Aimed to eliminate; no representation of ambiguity
10. **Integration:** *Lingua universalis* and *calculus ratiocinator* never fully unified
