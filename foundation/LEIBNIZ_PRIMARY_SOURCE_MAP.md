## Primary Texts by Leibniz (Chronological)

### 1. *Dissertatio de Arte Combinatoria* (1666)
- **Date:** 1666 (written age 20, doctoral dissertation)
- **Manuscript/Publication:** Published 1666; republished 1690 with additions
- **Terminology:** *ars combinatoria*, *combinatio*, *complexio*, *variatio*, *ordinatio*
- **Stated Objective:** A general method for discovering all possible combinations of concepts; "an art of combination" applicable to all sciences
- **Implicit Objective:** Foundational work toward a universal calculus of concepts
- **Ontology:** Concepts as basic units; complex concepts formed by combination of simpler ones
- **Conception of Concepts:** Concepts have intension (comprehension) and extension; simple concepts are indefinable primitives
- **Conception of Signs:** Symbols represent concepts; algebraic notation for logical operations
- **Conception of Composition:** Combination of concepts via multiplication-like operation; L × M = concept containing both
- **Conception of Truth:** Truth as identity of concepts in a proposition (subject-predicate)
- **Conception of Inference:** Substitution of equivalent concepts; syllogistic reasoning via combinatorial calculus
- **Treatment of Ambiguity:** Not systematically addressed; assumes concepts can be made precise
- **Primitive Concepts:** Alphabet of human thought; simple, indefinable concepts
- **Definitions:** Genus + differentia; recursive decomposition to primitives
- **Language:** Latin; symbolic notation inspired by algebra
- **Computation:** Mechanical calculation of combinations; anticipates calculating machine
- **Limitations:** No formal semantics; no treatment of relations; combinatorial explosion; no distinction between logical and grammatical combination
- **Unresolved Problems:** How to identify true primitives; how to handle relational concepts; how to avoid combinatorial explosion; how to connect to natural language

### 2. Early Writings on *Lingua Universalis* (1670s)
- **Key Texts:** *Elementa Characteristicae Universalis* (c. 1678-79), *Lingua Generalis* (1678)
- **Terminology:** *characteristica universalis*, *lingua generalis*, *real character*, *alphabet of human thought*
- **Stated Objective:** A universal language where signs directly represent concepts; "real characters" instead of arbitrary words
- **Implicit Objective:** Eliminate ambiguity of natural language; enable mechanical reasoning
- **Ontology:** Hierarchical classification of all concepts (inspired by Wilkins/Dalgarno)
- **Conception of Concepts:** Concepts as mental entities; classification by genus and differentia
- **Conception of Signs:** Each concept gets a unique character; characters composed from primitive elements
- **Conception of Composition:** Chemical-style notation: primitives combine to form complex concepts
- **Conception of Truth:** Proposition true when predicate concept contained in subject concept
- **Conception of Inference:** Substitution and elimination; calculus of substitution
- **Treatment of Ambiguity:** Aim to eliminate via precise definitions and unique characters
- **Primitive Concepts:** Small set of indefinables (≈ 100-200); "alphabet of human thought"
- **Definitions:** Recursive analysis to primitives
- **Language:** Artificial symbolic language; no natural language grammar
- **Computation:** Characteristic numbers assigned to concepts; arithmetic of concepts
- **Limitations:** Classification never completed; primitive set never fixed; no syntax for complex propositions
- **Unresolved Problems:** Complete taxonomy of concepts; fixed primitive basis; syntax for propositions; handling of syncategorematic terms

### 3. *Generales Inquisitiones de Analysi Notionum et Veritatum* (1686)
- **Date:** 1686
- **Manuscript/Publication:** Unpublished in Leibniz's lifetime; first published 1840 (Erdmann)
- **Terminology:** *notio* (concept), *veritas* (truth), *analisis*, *infinite analysis*, *contingentia*, *necessitas*
- **Stated Objective:** General inquiry into the analysis of concepts and truth
- **Implicit Objective:** Metaphysical foundation for the characteristica; theory of contingent truth
- **Ontology:** Concepts as possible worlds; each individual substance expresses the universe
- **Conception of Concepts:** Complete individual concepts contain all predicates (past, present, future); infinite analysis for contingent truths
- **Conception of Signs:** Signs as expressions of concepts; symbolic representation of infinite analysis
- **Conception of Composition:** Predicate-in-subject containment; infinite for contingent, finite for necessary
- **Conception of Truth:** "In every true affirmative proposition, necessary or contingent, universal or singular, the notion of the predicate is contained in some way in that of the subject" (praedicatum inest subjecto)
- **Conception of Inference:** Reduction to identities via analysis; principle of sufficient reason
- **Treatment of Ambiguity:** Ambiguity in finite analysis of contingent truths; resolved only in infinite analysis
- **Primitive Concepts:** Not explicitly listed; God's understanding contains complete concepts
- **Definitions:** Nominal vs. real definitions; real definitions require complete analysis
- **Language:** Latin philosophical prose; some symbolic notation
- **Computation:** Infinite analysis as limiting process; not mechanically computable
- **Limitations:** Infinite analysis not mechanizable; no decision procedure for contingent truths
- **Unresolved Problems:** How to finitely represent infinite analysis; decision procedure for contingency; connection to symbolic calculus

### 4. *Fundamenta Calculi Ratiocinatoris* (c. 1700-1710)
- **Date:** Various fragments c. 1700-1710
- **Manuscript/Publication:** Posthumous; scattered in manuscripts
- **Terminology:** *calculus ratiocinator*, *characteristic numbers*, *addition*, *multiplication* of concepts
- **Stated Objective:** A calculus for reasoning; mechanical deduction
- **Implicit Objective:** Algorithmic logic; decision procedure for (at least) syllogistic reasoning
- **Ontology:** Concepts as terms with characteristic numbers
- **Conception of Concepts:** Intensional; concepts have marks (primitive components)
- **Conception of Signs:** Numerical signs; each primitive gets a prime number
- **Conception of Composition:** Multiplication of characteristic numbers; A contains B iff num(A) divisible by num(B)
- **Conception of Truth:** Divisibility relation; true iff predicate number divides subject number
- **Conception of Inference:** Arithmetic of divisibility; syllogisms as numerical operations
- **Treatment of Ambiguity:** Numerical representation eliminates ambiguity
- **Primitive Concepts:** Assigned prime numbers (2, 3, 5, 7, 11...)
- **Definitions:** Product of primes = concept; factorization = analysis
- **Language:** Numerical notation
- **Computation:** Arithmetic operations; mechanizable
- **Limitations:** Only works for intensional containment; no negation, disjunction, relations, quantification; prime assignment arbitrary
- **Unresolved Problems:** Extension to full logic; treatment of negation; relational concepts; existential import

### 5. Writings on *Scientia Generalis* (1670s-1680s)
- **Key Texts:** *De Scientia Generali* (c. 1677), *Specimen Demonstrationis Politicae* (1671)
- **Terminology:** *scientia generalis*, *mathesis universalis*, *ars iudicandi*, *ars inveniendi*
- **Stated Objective:** A general science of reasoning and discovery; universal mathematics
- **Implicit Objective:** Unify all knowledge under a single method
- **Ontology:** All sciences reducible to combinatorial analysis of concepts
- **Conception of Concepts:** Universal concepts applicable across domains
- **Conception of Signs:** Universal notation for all sciences
- **Conception of Composition:** Universal combinatorial principles
- **Conception of Truth:** Universal criteria (identity, non-contradiction, sufficient reason)
- **Conception of Inference:** Universal rules of inference
- **Treatment of Ambiguity:** Resolved by universal method
- **Primitive Concepts:** Universal primitives (being, unity, number, etc.)
- **Definitions:** Universal definitional standards
- **Language:** Universal symbolic language
- **Computation:** Universal calculus
- **Limitations:** Never fully realized; remained programmatic
- **Unresolved Problems:** Complete system never built; relation to particular sciences unclear

### 6. Correspondence on Universal Language
- **Key Correspondents:** Huygens, Oldenburg, Tschirnhaus, Lambert, Ploucquet, Kastner
- **Dates:** 1670s-1716
- **Relevant Letters:**
  - To Oldenburg (1676): On Wilkins's *Essay* and real character
  - To Tschirnhaus (1679): On classification of concepts
  - To Lambert (1700s): On calculus ratiocinator and characteristic numbers
  - To Ploucquet (1760s): On logical calculus (Ploucquet's algebraic logic)
- **Themes:** Critique of Wilkins/Dalgarno; search for primitive concepts; numerical calculus; mechanical reasoning

### 7. Metaphysical Writings Relevant to Architecture
- **Discourse on Metaphysics (1686):** Complete individual concepts; predicate-in-subject; sufficient reason
- **Monadology (1714):** Simple substances; pre-established harmony; expression
- **New System (1695):** Substantial forms; vinculum substantiale
- **Primary Truths (1689):** Identity of indiscernibles; principle of sufficient reason; analysis of concepts

## Evolution Map

| Period | Focus | Key Innovation | Unresolved |
|--------|-------|----------------|------------|
| 1666 | *Ars Combinatoria* | Combinatorial generation of concepts | Primitives undefined; no semantics |
| 1670s | *Lingua Universalis* | Real characters; hierarchical classification | Taxonomy incomplete; primitives not fixed |
| 1686 | *Generales Inquisitiones* | Infinite analysis; predicate-in-subject truth | Infinite analysis non-mechanical; contingency |
| 1690s | *Calculus Ratiocinator* | Characteristic numbers; arithmetic of concepts | Only positive containment; no negation/relations |
| 1700s | Fragments | Numerical decision procedure for syllogisms | Fragmentary; never integrated with lingua universalis |

## Distinction: Leibniz's Proposals vs. Later Interpretations

### Leibniz Actually Proposed:
- Combinatorial art for concept generation (1666)
- Real characters representing concepts directly (1670s)
- Predicate-in-subject theory of truth (1686)
- Characteristic numbers with prime factorization (c. 1700)
- Infinite analysis for contingent truths (1686)
- Universal science unifying all knowledge (1670s)

### Later Interpretations (NOT Leibniz):
- "Leibniz anticipated modern symbolic logic" — partially true but anachronistic
- "Leibniz invented predicate logic" — no; he had intensional containment, not Fregean quantification
- "Leibniz's calculus ratiocinator = modern computer" — metaphorical; he envisioned mechanical reasoning but not stored-program computation
- "Leibniz had a complete universal language" — false; he never completed the taxonomy or fixed the primitives
- "Leibniz solved the problem of contingency" — false; infinite analysis is not a decision procedure
- "Characteristic numbers = Gödel numbering" — analogy only; Leibniz used for divisibility, not syntax encoding

## Primary Source Locations

### Critical Editions:
- **Academy Edition (Leibniz-Arbeitsstelle Hannover):** Series I-VIII — definitive
- **Gerhardt (1849-63):** *Die philosophischen Schriften von Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz* — 7 vols, still used
- **Fichant (1991):** *Écrits logiques* — French edition of logical fragments
- **Lenzen (1990, 2004):** *Leibniz: Logical Papers* — English translations
- **Swoyer (1995):** *Leibniz on Combinatorics* — *Dissertatio* translation

### Digital Access:
- **Leibniz Digital (leibniz-digital.de):** High-res scans of manuscripts
- **Project Gutenberg:** Theodicy, Monadology, New Essays (English)
- **Early English Books Online:** Latin originals
- **Gallica (BnF):** French translations
