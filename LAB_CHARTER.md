# Characteristica Research Lab

## Mission

Characteristica Research Lab investigates whether historical and modern attempts at universal representation can inform a useful model-independent semantic intermediate representation for AI systems.

The lab is not attempting to "solve universal language" in one expedition.

Its immediate objective is to determine whether there is a genuine, experimentally accessible research frontier between human natural language and machine-facing representations.

## Core Research Hypothesis

Natural-language prompting may be an inefficient interface for communicating human intent to AI systems.

A structured semantic intermediate representation may preserve task meaning while improving one or more of:

- token efficiency
- inference cost
- latency
- consistency
- compositionality
- machine interpretability
- cross-model portability
- formal reasoning
- tool interoperability

## Historical Scope

The lab studies relevant predecessors before Leibniz, Leibniz deeply, important post-Leibniz systems, and modern technical analogues.

Historical research is only useful when it yields a transferable mechanism, design principle, failure mode, or experimental hypothesis.

## Scientific Principles

1. Evidence over enthusiasm.
2. Reproducibility over anecdotes.
3. Falsification is valuable.
4. Negative results are valid results.
5. Historical claims require sources.
6. Novelty claims require prior-art review.
7. Experimental claims require baselines.
8. Compression must account for decoding and translation overhead.
9. No system is "universal" merely because it handles several examples.
10. Agents may propose discoveries but may not declare them established.

## Allowed Finding Labels

All findings must be classified as one of:

- Observation
- Historical Claim
- Hypothesis
- Experimental Result
- Potential Novelty
- Known Prior Art
- Unresolved
- Falsified
- Candidate Contribution

Only the Research Director may mark a result as Candidate Contribution, and only after prior-art and red-team review.

## Lab Verdicts

Each expedition must end in one of:

### GREEN — Pursue
Strong enough theoretical or experimental evidence exists to justify substantial additional research.

### AMBER — Continue Narrowly
Interesting signal exists, but evidence is incomplete, scope is narrower than expected, or further validation is required.

### RED — Discontinue
The central hypothesis provides no meaningful advantage, is already substantially solved, or fails under experimental scrutiny.

A RED verdict is considered a successful research outcome if it is well supported.

## Anti-Goals

The lab must avoid:

- writing a general history of philosophy
- producing biographies instead of mechanisms
- equating short prompts with semantic efficiency
- benchmarking only favorable examples
- claiming novelty without searching prior work
- hiding complexity in compilers or adapters
- optimizing exclusively for token count
- mistaking structured prompting for a universal representation
- allowing agents to duplicate large amounts of work
- treating LLM agreement as evidence of truth
